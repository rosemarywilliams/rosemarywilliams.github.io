About

Painting and poetry from the same attentive place.

Rosemary Williams works across visual art and poetry, bringing together image, rhythm, and close observation. Her pieces often begin with what is near at hand: a shift in light, a remembered landscape, a face, a garden, a room.

The studio practice is quiet and cumulative. Paintings gather color and texture; poems gather sound and image. Both forms ask for patience, and both make room for feeling without rushing it.

Studio note

"The page and the canvas are both places to notice what has almost disappeared."

What to expect

Work with texture, restraint, and feeling.

Observation

A sustained attention to ordinary details and natural shifts.

Memory

Work that holds personal and shared histories without over-explaining them.

Language

Poems that sit beside the visual work as another form of seeing.
